## 1. Premise

You run accounts receivable, month-end close and audit support for the same cookware exporter. You
see every other department's shortcuts arrive as numbers: backdated invoices, padded forecasts,
missing receipts. Your job is to make the books true, and the people who send you the numbers would
often prefer that you make them convenient.

## 2. What the player should feel

- The books are where every other department's small lie ends up.
- Closing the month creates hard deadlines and social pressure to "make it balance".
- Being the last control is lonely; an auditor will ask who knew.
- Small adjustments are how large misstatements begin.

## 3. Arc (outline)

Learn the close (1 to 12); first favour for a colleague (13 to 26); the favours compound into a problem (27 to 40); audit and year-end (41 to 52).

## 5. Timeline (proposals)

Nov: audit fieldwork; Dec: year-end close, accruals, bonus calculations.

## 6. Arc ideas

- **The Month-End Push:** a sales manager asks you to book a shipment early; escalates quarter by quarter.
- **The Receipt Problem:** expenses without receipts from a senior person; you decide between documenting, querying and accepting.
- **The Audit:** what the auditor finds traces back to your own year.

## 7. Dark-side ladder (proposals)

Accepting a missing receipt; rounding accruals; booking revenue early (cut-off); hiding an overdue
receivable; personal expense booked as business; creating a reserve to smooth profit ("cookie jar");
falsifying a bank reconciliation.
