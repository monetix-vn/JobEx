# Series bible: Brand and Digital Marketing Executive (tenth job; fourth of the five departments added on 2026-10-02)

Status: DRAFT v1 by AI (2026-10-02), asked for by the owner ("then marketing"), written together with the
scenes. Not reviewed by the owner or a practitioner. Playable at `?role=mkt`. Same cookware company as
the other factory jobs; marketing sits in the head office and owns the box, the website, the reviews, the
agency and the social posts. Tone: `content/industry-cookware/events/README.md`. Format:
`docs/AUTHORING.md`. Advertising, review and data-protection rules in the scenes are generic and
approximate; they need review by a practitioner who knows current advertising practice.

## 1. Premise

You run the brand and the digital campaigns for a cookware company. The product is good, the box is the
first thing a buyer reads, and the competitor's box says something bigger. A launch date does not wait for
the test results, a rating of three point eight does not sell, and the agency has a very generous idea of how
to thank you. Every claim you make is a promise that QC, customer service and, one day, an inspector will
read back to you.

## 2. What the player should feel

- A claim is a promise to every buyer; the evidence is what turns it from a hope into a fact.
- A fake review, an unlabelled sponsored post and a staged testimonial are the same lie in three costumes.
- A reprint costs money, and a recall costs the brand; the cheap moment to be honest is before the pallets leave.
- An agency that gives gifts is asking for something; an invoice for work not delivered is a payment for nothing.
- The people who hear the complaints (customer service) and the people who run the tests (QC) are your allies.

## 3. The player's arc

1. Weeks 1 to 12: learn the claims register, the brand and the agency.
2. Weeks 13 to 26: the first overclaim, the first review problem, the first agency invoice.
3. Weeks 27 to 40: the launch twist, the test result, the box, the influencer, the trip.
4. Weeks 41 to 52: the consumer inspection, the complaints, the review and the offer (or the fall).

## 7. Dark-side ladder

A borrowed photo -> a comparison on mismatched conditions -> a paid award shown as earned -> a budget
spent to keep it -> a countdown timer that resets -> a staged testimonial -> a bought list nobody agreed to
join -> an unlabelled paid post -> a green leaf on products with nothing recycled -> an invoice approved
for work not delivered -> a trip from the agency told to nobody -> a lifetime claim on a box the tests do
not support -> a bad test result kept until after the launch -> fake reviews and a rival's reviews reported
-> a defect blamed on customers -> the register filled in after the claim and the reviews deleted before
an inspection.

## 8. Endings

Completed, burnout, fired, prosecuted (engine); promoted to marketing manager (`mkt.manager_offer`);
walked away (`mkt.resignation_thought`, or declining the promotion).
