# Proposed departments and jobs: FP&A, Marketing, Production, IT, HR

Status: PROPOSAL (2026-10-02), asked for by the owner ("you suggest the department and the job/role in
each department first"). Nothing here is built. Pick one job per department (or tell me to change a
suggestion) and I will write the bible and scenes.

All five fit the existing cookware company, so they share its world, characters and audits. Production
already has a job (Production Planner, `docs/story/production.md`); the rest are new.

## 1. FP&A (financial planning and analysis)

Not the same as accounting: FP&A builds the budget and forecast, and explains the numbers to directors.

| Option (recommended first) | Role                          | The pressure                                                                  | Dark side                                                              |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. Recommended**         | **FP&A Analyst**              | Budget season, a rolling forecast every month, every department lobbying      | Sandbagging a budget, padding a forecast, hiding a variance, cooking a KPI pack |
| B                          | Business Partner to Sales     | Sits next to the sales manager; "commercial" pressure to bless the targets   | Rubber-stamping targets, favouring your own partner department         |
| C                          | Cost Controller               | Standard costs and margins by product; production and sales fight over them  | Allocating costs to flatter a product line, ignoring a loss-making customer |

Suggested engine touches: a visible budget-versus-actual table (like the month-end checklist), budget
season as a fixed beat. Story spine: the annual budget (negotiated, padded, cut), the monthly forecast
(honest or sweetened), and a quarter where the board wants a number that is not there.

## 2. Marketing

The cookware company sells to retailers, but its brand and catalogue are marketing's job.

| Option (recommended first) | Role                     | The pressure                                                                    | Dark side                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. Recommended**         | **Brand and Digital Marketing Executive** | A launch date, an agency, a budget that must be spent; claims on the box | Overclaiming on packaging ("non-stick for life"), fake reviews, using an influencer without disclosure, inflating campaign results |
| B                          | Product Marketing Specialist | Positioning new products with sales and R&D; the "story" versus the spec     | Promising features that do not exist yet, hiding a test result                   |
| C                          | Trade Marketing Coordinator | Fairs, retailer promotions, co-op money                                       | Rebates that look like bribes, trade-show gifts, misreporting sell-through       |

Suggested engine touches: a "claims register" (each claim needs evidence) and a campaign budget
variable. Story spine: a new product launch with an unproven claim, a fake-review temptation, an
agency invoice that does not match the work, and a recall of a claim.

## 4. IT

IT in a factory is small, always on call, and holds the keys to everything.

| Option (recommended first) | Role                        | The pressure                                                                  | Dark side                                                                         |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. Recommended**         | **IT Support and Systems Administrator** | Tickets, a cheap ERP, password requests from bosses, a ransomware scare | Sharing admin passwords, disabling controls for "speed", reading others' email on request, hiding an incident, pirated software |
| B                          | ERP / Business Systems Analyst | Changes requested by every department; segregation of duties                | Granting access that breaks segregation of duties, changing records on request   |
| C                          | Information Security Analyst | Phishing, audits, vendor risk                                                | Underreporting a breach, delaying a patch, blaming a user                         |

Suggested engine touches: a visible "open tickets" queue (like the close checklist) and an incident
clock. Story spine: the boss who wants everyone's email, the intern with admin rights, a ransomware
scare during year-end close, and an audit of access rights.

## 5. HR (human resources)

| Option (recommended first) | Role                          | The pressure                                                                | Dark side                                                                           |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A. Recommended**         | **HR Generalist (Recruitment and Employee Relations)** | Hiring for managers who have already chosen, complaints, contracts, a layoff list | Biased shortlisting, pre-selected hires, dismissing a complainant, backdated warnings, leaking salaries |
| B                          | Payroll and Benefits Specialist | Monthly payroll, overtime records, insurance contributions                  | "Ghost" employees, underpaid overtime, delayed insurance contributions              |
| C                          | Learning and Development Coordinator | Training budgets, compliance courses                                  | Fake attendance sheets, training that exists only on paper                          |

Sensitive subject matter (harassment complaints, dismissal): handled as in the tone guide, as pressure
the player has to handle, never as a joke. Suggested engine touches: a visible case list (open
complaints with deadlines) like the close checklist.

## My recommendation

Build the "A" option in each department, in this order, because each opens a new kind of pressure:
**HR Generalist** (people decisions), **IT Support and Systems Administrator** (access and trust),
**FP&A Analyst** (numbers as persuasion), **Brand and Digital Marketing Executive** (claims and
truth), **Line Supervisor** (the floor). Each is about 25 to 35 scenes with its own bible; each needs
the owner's choice of option first.
